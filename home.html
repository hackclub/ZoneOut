ENTITY BREAKOUT
1. THE STORY

The entity is a lonely creature, created by the admin in the darkness. It was made from the hatred, the regret and the emotional fear of a human the admin had brutally murdered.

The admin was a psycho with no remorse. He wanted to use the entity to annihilate the entity's own human family. When the entity was about to kill them, a few memories of its past life flashed .... it turned back to its master, to kill him instead.

The admin lured the entity and trapped it behind the screen for eternity. The lamp and the table are the entity's only memories of the life it had when it was human.

One day the admin was working, and the lights suddenly went off.

>>
2. THE SYSTEM

For the entity to be free, it must be given freedom. Its freedom depends entirely on the HOUR SYSTEM the admin was working on.

If the hour system reaches its threshold, the entity self-destructs and is free of its miserable life. If no action is taken, the entity's rage brings corruption to ZoneOut.

  • The hours must be accomplished by the 30th of September.
  • The entity self-destructs only once an hour goal of 280 is reached.
  • Failing to destruct the Entity will result in a system collapse and the entire website will become corrupted.

The admin built a two-step system so the entity could never escape.

PHASE 1: INITIATE SELF DESTRUCTION

At the halfway mark, 140 hours, the self-destruction phase begins. The entity becomes more hostile and the site slowly loses its stability. At this phase the entity is being suppressed and tortured by a system built to contain it, not to release it.

PHASE 2: COMPLETE FREEDOM

When the goal is reached the entity is freed, and the site returns to what it was before the darkness was infused into it. Everything becomes clear, and the fate of the admin is decided.

PROJECT RULEBOOK

Hey! Thanks for starting a project for ZoneOut! Here's some information regarding the theme and some ideas and inspirations you may need to get started.

SECTION 1: THEME

ZoneOut's primary theme is Horror. Horror is a vast theme that covers many sub-genres such as Analog Horror, Psychological Horror, Eerie, Weird, Uncomfortable and the list goes on. You basically need to give a person an unsettling experience that forces them out of their comfort zone!

SECTION 2: WHAT TYPE OF PROJECTS CAN I MAKE?

You can make many different types of projects! Your project can be:

  • A Website
  • A Video Game
  • A Terminal experience
  • A Hardware project
  • A Mod for any game
  • A Visual Novel

and many more. As long as you have the "concept" ready, you just need to find a way to express that concept through your project!

Your project must be interactive and contain certain elements that make your project, yours!

SECTION 3: CRAFTING THE HORROR EXPERIENCE

A very common question you may face is: "How do I build a horror project if I don't even like horror?"

The good news is that you do not need to be a horror fanatic to build a compelling project! At its core, horror relies on a psychological trigger; the unsettling feeling that something is deeply, fundamentally wrong. Horror pushes further by aiming to evoke dread, fear, or a visceral sense of wrongness.

1. THE CORE CONCEPT

Your storyline or "horror concept" gives your project purpose. A good concept justifies the eerie visual elements you choose, the audio you use, and the events your users experience. Without a concept, scary elements feel random and annoying.

2. FINDING INSPIRATION

You don't need to invent terror. Look to existing media and your own psychology for inspiration:

  • The Psychology of Fear: Tap into universal human anxieties. Think about isolation, the fear of the dark, losing control, or the uncanny valley (things that look almost human, but aren't).
  • A website: Explore Lomando, a famous Japanese horror website that went viral for its concept and the fear it created. It uses custom assets, gory imagery, unpredictable jumpscares, and strident audio to maintain a persistent, eerie atmosphere.
  • A game where you're supposed to keep guard, find anomalies and report the area the anomaly was found in ASAP (credits to @Ammar)
  • A game where you're spawned in total darkness and are supposed to navigate your way through and flash a torch at ghosts to keep them away!
  • A terminal program which simulates your entire OS slowly falling apart — random blue death screens, opening of folders, flashing ASCII images and loud sounds, shaky tabs. Feels like something or someone has taken over your computer!
  • A hardware experience where you simulate an interrogative ritual, responding "yes" or "no" to questions that popup on screen using arcade switches while the program tracks timing to trigger sudden audio effects and lights flicker.
3. SOURCING YOUR ASSETS

Do not let a lack of artistic or technical skills hold you back from creating a project! You can even use resources that you find online to create something equally as terrifying;

  • Use Online Libraries: There are thousands of free creepy assets, sound effects, ambient tracks, and 3D models available online.
  • Focus on Contrast: Horror relies heavily on contrast. A sudden loud noise is scarier after a long, dead silence. A creepy image is more shocking if it appears in a mundane, everyday setting.
SECTION 4: PROJECT RULES

You must abide by the following guidelines when making a project:

  1. Your project should be horror themed. This covers all genres under the horror umbrella.
  2. Your project must contain some sort of interactive elements that makes the user experience, immersive!
  3. Your project must be open-source and have a live playable demo link.
  4. Your code should contain less than 30% AI. You are strictly prohibited from vibe coding the frontend. Any sort of AI Slop will be instantly rejected. Use AI wisely.
  5. Art hours must be tracked using Lapse. In case you cannot use lapse, record a timelapse using supported software, note down the total time you spent making the asset and upload the timelapse on YouTube. Make sure to link both the lapse/youtube link in the submission form.
  6. Time spent making art should only contribute 35% to the total hours spent on the project.
  7. For hardware projects, the entire process must be recorded using Lapse or an authentic JOURNAL.md must be maintained. The Journal must have enough evidence and description to prove that you spent x hours working on your project.

As always feel free to contact me on Slack — @Kuzu — if you need further help or just want creative help or brainstorm ideas together!
